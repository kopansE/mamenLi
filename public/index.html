How it works

Two sides, one garment

If you are a brand

You are buying a position, not a post

  1. Browse the publishers and open the one whose room you want to be in.
  2. Pick a numbered spot on the front or the back. Each one shows what it costs right now.
  3. Bid your maximum. We bid the least you need to hold it and never more than your number.
  4. Upload your mark, pay by card, and it is printed on the garment for the day.

If you are a publisher

Your garment is the only line that can earn

  1. Photograph yourself front and back in what you are going to wear.
  2. Drag out the spots on the picture. Fourteen is typical; the front is worth more.
  3. Set a floor under each one and a goal for the whole thing.
  4. Open the bidding. You are paid out after the day, less the platform fee.

FAQ

Questions, answered

What if the spot I want is already taken?
Take it off them. Every spot stays contestable until it closes: bid more than the standing price plus 15% and it is yours. The brand that held it is refunded in full, automatically, and told it happened. Nothing is locked until the clock runs out.
How does bidding your maximum work?
You state the most you are willing to pay. The spot then sits at the least you need to hold it — not at your maximum. If someone bids under your ceiling, your price rises just enough to stay ahead and you keep the spot. If someone goes over it, you are outbid and refunded. You are never charged more than the number you typed, and bidding your true ceiling early costs you nothing.
When am I actually charged?
Your card is authorised when you bid, which places a hold rather than a charge. If you are outbid, the hold is released and nothing is ever taken. If you are still holding the spot when it closes, the hold is captured and you are charged the settled price — which is often less than your maximum.
What if the goal is not reached?
Everybody is refunded in full. The goal is the number the publisher needs for the whole thing to be worth printing; below it, the campaign does not run and nobody is charged.
Can a bid be sniped in the last second?
No. Any bid inside the final ten minutes pushes that spot's close out by ten minutes. A spot only closes once nobody has bid for ten clear minutes, so the last bid is always one somebody had a chance to answer.
What format does my logo need to be?
SVG or a PNG on transparency, at least 1000px on the long edge. Solid single-colour marks print best on fabric. Upload it when you bid and it appears on the garment straight away.
